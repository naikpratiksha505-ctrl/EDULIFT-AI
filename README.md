# EduLift AI — AI-Powered Student Analytics and Success Platform

> **Every Student Deserves a Chance to Succeed.**

EduLift AI is a student-support platform designed to identify academic challenges early and encourage timely, personalized support. It helps students and faculty understand academic performance, monitor attendance, plan studies, and identify areas where additional guidance may be helpful.

🌐 **Live Website:** https://merry-mermaid-c52a5b.netlify.app/

---

## 📌 About the Project

Students may face academic difficulties because of attendance gaps, challenging subjects, or poor study planning. Often, these problems are noticed only when examination results decline.

EduLift AI aims to address this challenge by providing academic insights and early-support recommendations. It helps students understand where they can improve and helps faculty mentors identify students who may benefit from additional guidance.

The goal is not to label students as weak or unsuccessful, but to provide the right support at the right time.

## 🎯 Problem Statement

Traditional academic monitoring often focuses on examination results rather than identifying difficulties early.

This can lead to:
- Students struggling with specific subjects without timely support.
- Attendance problems going unnoticed.
- Difficulty creating effective study schedules.
- Faculty discovering academic difficulties too late.
- Limited personalized guidance for individual students.

## 💡 Proposed Solution

EduLift AI provides a centralized platform for student academic insights and early intervention.

It aims to help users:
- Monitor academic performance and attendance.
- Identify subjects that may require additional attention.
- Receive personalized study recommendations.
- Understand why an academic alert was generated.
- Support faculty mentorship and follow-up.

## ✨ Key Features

### 📊 1. Student Analytics
Provides an overview of academic indicators to help students understand their progress and identify areas for improvement.

### 🤖 2. AI-Powered Insights
Uses academic indicators to support early identification of students who may benefit from assistance. Predictions should be treated as estimates, not guaranteed outcomes.

### 📚 3. Personalized Study Planning
Helps students organize their study time and focus on topics that need additional practice.

### 🏫 4. Attendance Monitoring
Highlights attendance patterns that may require attention and follow-up.

### 👩‍🏫 5. Faculty Mentorship
Helps faculty review student-support alerts and identify opportunities for timely academic guidance.

### 💬 6. Explainable Insights
Aims to make recommendations understandable by showing the academic indicators behind an alert.

### 🌱 7. Early Intervention
Encourages timely support through mentoring, additional practice, and personalized study plans before difficulties grow.

## 👥 Target Users

- **Students:** To understand their academic progress and improve study habits.
- **Faculty Members:** To identify students who may need additional support.
- **Educational Institutions:** To encourage proactive academic monitoring and student success.

## ⚙️ How It Works

1. **Review academic indicators:** Consider available marks, attendance, and other relevant information.
2. **Identify potential challenges:** Highlight patterns that may indicate a need for support.
3. **Generate insights:** Present understandable explanations and recommendations.
4. **Suggest next steps:** Encourage focused study, practice, or a faculty check-in.
5. **Support improvement:** Review progress and adjust support as needed.

## 🔒 Privacy and Responsible AI

EduLift AI is designed to support students, not judge them.

- AI predictions can be incorrect and should be reviewed by a human.
- An alert should encourage support rather than define a student's abilities.
- Student information should be protected and accessible only to authorized users.
- Real student data should be used only with appropriate permissions and safeguards.
- Recommendations should be fair, understandable, and open to correction.
